Being Okay with Being Alone

May 10, 2025

"Congratulations, you are one of our chosen Bakti Champions!"

The end of the tunnel.

I had been rejected from multiple scholarships before I was accepted into Central Asia Bank's scholarship for undergraduate students.

I was in a Burger King when I got the email, and I thought to myself, "Huh, another rejection", before I opened it, only to find out that it was an acceptance letter. I ran outside to call my parents.

But did I deserve it?

I mean, they accepted my slightly half-assed application. I mean, when they requested competition certificates, I submitted my TOEFL certificate. The certificate I paid 60k to take online. So clearly, they had looked at me and thousands of other applicants and thought, "Yes, this one. Put her in.".

But do I think I deserved it? I mean, sure. I wouldn't say I'm a bad student, would you?

Let me ask you to look around. The students without scholarships, and students with scholarships. Do you see any undeserving students? Any deserving students who for now sits in the "no-scholarship" group? I have a friend who hosts study sessions every Tuesday nights. She does NOT have a scholarship. She is active in organizations, participates in competitions, has pretty good public speaking skills, and yet, no scholarship has accepted her yet. Finances wise, she definitely qualifies for monetary aid. So why hasn't she been accepted?

I think the answer that I found after looking at the accepted students in my scholarship community and others, is that the people who were accepted are people who have the grit to keep, keep, keep applying and applying. When I look at my community, the people around me, and I ask "what do we all have in common, that they accepted us as awardees?" Grit, and will. We all had a will. Where there's a will, there's a way, right?

Is that to say my friend didn't have a will? No, absolutely not. I'd say she's one of the ones with the most will. Most grit. She's a very strong woman. Perhaps for her circumstances and the cards she'd been dealt, she needs more grit than most.

What of those who lie, then?

This is such a topic, right? Those who lie, when they succeed, what does that mean? It just means they bypassed the need for grit and strength using lies. That's why they were accepted. I think, anyways. Who knows.
