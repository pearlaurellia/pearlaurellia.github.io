Women & Vanity

May 10, 2025

Why do you think women care so much about their appearances? Why is this so widely done, that it is considered a stereotype?

My Experience with Being "Ugly"

Before you read this, keep in mind that everything written here are mere observations, empirical ones, experienced first hand by myself.

I was never really the type of girl to care much about her appearance. I didn't really try. I fought acne for most of my pre-teen years, and even then, I didn't have the strong will to keep fighting it in order to look "pretty". I went through the motions mainly because my parents wanted my acne gone. And for the most part, I didn't care about my appearance at all. And then, I attended a boarding school, where I lived with around 50 other girls until graduation, and then I became well-acquainted with something called insecurity, or self-consciousness.

I won't make anything up or exaggerate. I was very well-known in high school due to my academic achievements. Parallel rankings, always in the top 3, competed to quality into regionals twice for the national astronomy olympiads. I brought home a trophy to decorate those grand, wooden case, displayed proudly in the school's lobby. My face was on a big poster, hung where everyone could see. I was nicknamed the "Jack of All Trades" by the vice headmaster. So I won't sit here and tell you that my high school days were completely miserable, but I noticed things.

After all the achievements I listed, you might ask me why in the world I'd care about something so vain, when I'm clearly gifted in other ways. I guess I am, still, just a girl. Is that enough of a reason?

I hated my appearance in high school. I didn't have many pictures - but I took many, because I refused to be the subject, so I became the photographer. I was never the type of girl to post my face on my Instagram stories because I never had a good enough picture. In contexts where academics aren't involved, I was overlooked for the most vain reason, and it hurt so bad for that exact reason.

"So? Do something about it, then."

Well, I did. I ran laps every afternoon, and 100 jumping jacks were (are) the absolute minimum for any post-running session. That, paired with planks and sit-ups because every pretty girl has a flat tummy. I graduated high school with 55 kg. I entered college, and after my first semester, I was down to 47 kg. Now, I am consistently 46-47 kg. Have I mentioned my increasingly complex makeup and haircare routine?

Let's backtrack for a second. How did I lose all those weight? Simple. I had awful eating habits. Have you ever survived off of 4 spoonfuls of oatmeal a day? Well, I did, and I almost didn't. I still remember tumbling as I try to walk and get up. I ignored every red flag, every warning sign, and I continued dieting. I was absolutely miserable, but I lost the weight.

My makeup routine has gotten very, very complex. Skin prep, then this very specific powder I refuse to replace with anything else, lipstick, fake eyelashes, brown liquid liner, blush and eyeshadow, lipgloss. Hair care? Higher end shampoo and conditioner, also, my hair has her own perfume. I feel happy when I do this routine. I try not to think about why.

So to answer the title of this blog post: Women and Vanity, and why the two are linked together, I would say it's because of our environment and what it's normalized. Treating prettier girls a certain way while less prettier girls another way. Why do women do their makeup? Well, I can't speak for everyone, but I'm sure in a venn diagram about this, there's a good portion of women that do it to be taken seriously, and to be seen.
